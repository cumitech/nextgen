To protect your Moodle 5.2.2+ Online Learning Management System (LMS) from legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and payment disputes, your website must include four mandatory legal pages. Because you use a custom theme and a custom payment plugin, these pages must contain specific disclosures to address the increased risk of security vulnerabilities and financial disputes.

1. Privacy Policy

This is legally mandated by global laws (like GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and COPPA) if you collect any personal data.
• Moodle-Specific Requirements: You must state exactly what user data Moodle logs (IP addresses, course completion tracking, quiz attempts, and activity logs).
• Custom Theme Disclaimer: Disclose whether your custom theme utilizes third-party fonts (like Google Fonts) or tracking scripts that capture user behavior.
• Payment Plugin Disclosures: Clearly state that you do not store raw credit card data on your Moodle database (assuming compliance). Name the payment gateway used (in our context Campay) and link to their privacy policy.

2. Terms and Conditions (Terms of Service)

This forms a binding legal contract between you and your students/instructors to prevent platform abuse.
• Intellectual Property (IP): Explicitly state who owns the course content, videos, and materials. Forbid users from downloading, sharing, or reselling course assets.
• Custom Plugin Liability Limit: Include a clause stating the LMS is provided "as-is." Since you are using custom code (theme and payment plugins), this protects you if a bug in your code causes a system crash, data loss, or faulty transaction.
• Account Termination: Reserve the right to ban users who cheat, share accounts, or post abusive content in Moodle forums.

3. Refund and Cancellation Policy

Essential for preventing chargebacks and payment gateway merchant account suspensions.
• Clear Triggers: Define exactly when a student is eligible for a refund (e.g., "within 14 days of purchase provided less than 10% of the course has been accessed").
• Payment Failures: Address how disputes are handled if your custom payment plugin fails to automatically provision a course after a successful charge.

4. Accessibility Statement

Failing to accommodate disabled learners can trigger lawsuits under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) or the European Accessibility Act (EAA).
• Compliance Level: State your target compliance (e.g., WCAG 2.1 AA).
• Custom Theme Notice: Core Moodle is highly accessible, but custom themes often break accessibility (e.g., poor color contrast, broken screen-reader navigation). Test your theme and provide a dedicated contact email where users can report accessibility barriers.
